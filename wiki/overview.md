# Overview

## Question

How much faster is noisecirc than state-of-the-art GPU state-vector simulators of noisy circuits, and which part of the gain comes from its algorithm and which from its implementation? The focus is on gates whose noise is a non-mixed-unitary Kraus channel (such as amplitude damping), on qubits, `complex64`, noisecirc's exact sampling mode, and GPU state-vector simulators (PLAN.md).

## Current picture

noisecirc samples quantum trajectories by rejection: it proposes Kraus operators from state-independent upper bounds and accepts them by the norm of the propagated state, which lets it propose all shots before simulating, propagate each distinct trajectory once, and share trajectory prefixes through checkpoints, segment by segment and depth-first ([NoiseCirc's sampling algorithm](concepts/noisecirc-algorithm.md)). The literature check found the rejection-based exact sampling of non-mixed-unitary channels new; depth-first prefix reuse exists for Pauli noise, and wide shared-memory kernels exist without noise ([Prior art of noisecirc's algorithm](studies/prior-art.md)).

Its work is counted in block passes: launches of a fused block's kernel on one state vector. The passes follow a universal law in the expected errors per shot $M$, $P \approx BN[1/N + f(M)] + 2MN$ for amplitude damping, and noisecirc's own sampler, run on surrogate states without a GPU, predicts them to about 10% ([Block passes against noisecirc's parameters](studies/pass-count.md), [Block-pass cost model](concepts/block-pass-cost-model.md)).

On the H100, a run takes the passes times the bandwidth time of one state times 1.2 to 2.6 at noisecirc's defaults, and 1.3 to 1.8 with blocks of at most 32 gates; large blocks and the general (error) kernels are slower than the bandwidth on this GPU ([Block-pass time on the H100](studies/block-pass-time.md)). Against Qiskit Aer and CUDA-Q, noisecirc is 6 to 7700 times faster on amplitude damping, and the gain is algorithmic: wide fusion of noisy gates, sharing of trajectories and no norm passes, all enabled by proposing Kraus operators without the state; at equal algorithm the implementations are within a factor of 2 ([noisecirc against Qiskit Aer and CUDA-Q](studies/competitors.md)). Tuning Aer's options does not change this: its best setting, a fusion width of 3 instead of 2 qubits, saves 8% on average, about as much as noisecirc's own tuning, and noisecirc stays 6 to 48 times faster than Aer's shot branching with both tuned ([Tuning Qiskit Aer's shot branching](studies/aer-tuning.md)). The strongest per-shot competitor found, cuStateVec Ex with qsim's delayed lower-bound sampling, is still 4 to 520 times slower: lower bounds save the norm passes of a single trajectory, but only noisecirc's upper bounds with rejection let it share trajectories between shots, which is most of its gain at weak and intermediate noise ([noisecirc against delayed lower-bound sampling](studies/lower-bound-sampling.md)). Without running a circuit, its passes are predicted within 14% and its time within 33% (kernel-ratio model), as GPU runs of seven circuits and five channels confirm ([Benchmark circuits and predicted work](studies/benchmark-suite.md), [Validation of the predicted work](studies/validation.md)). Two performance issues of noisecirc were found and fixed on branches ([Issues found in noisecirc](studies/noisecirc-issues.md)). Beyond fixes, two improvements of noisecirc, masked kernels for trajectories with errors and per-circuit autotuning of block size and stage width, make it 6 to 25% faster on the H100 ([Error kernels and block sizes](studies/error-kernels.md)); its other parameters, tuned on an RTX 4060 Ti, are close to the best on the H100 too ([Tuning noisecirc's parameters on the H100](studies/parameter-tuning.md)). Tighter, state-dependent proposal bounds would save little: at most 6% of the passes with the information the passes provide ([Checkpoint-dependent proposal bounds](studies/tighter-bounds.md)). Which Kraus set noisecirc samples matters only for channels with a mixed-unitary part: its default (canonical) set already has the most dominant first operator, and the provably optimal set keeps that operator and minimises the oversampling of the others, with no trade-off between error proposals and accepted errors ([Optimal Kraus representations for noisecirc](studies/kraus-representation.md)). On the H100, a pass is bound by the instructions it issues, arithmetic and shared-memory accesses together, rather than by bandwidth; kernels that use the gates' structure issue 18 to 75% fewer arithmetic instructions, and together with 4-qubit stages and `complex32` storage they make whole runs 1.45 to 1.9 times faster, at an accuracy of about $10^{-3}$ ([Precision, block size and the instruction-bound pass](studies/kernel-regime.md), [Fewer instructions per block pass](studies/block-instructions.md)). Tensor cores do not help at single precision: the passes are already close to the memory floor, a stage applied as a dense matrix costs twice the operations of noisecirc's structured kernels, and recovering single precision from TF32 or FP16 inputs triples that, so tensor-core stages built into svpass are 15% to 2.6 times slower, and FP16 products gain only 4 to 8% on brickwork at an accuracy worse than `complex32` ([Tensor cores for block passes](studies/tensor-cores.md)). Of the ideas from a brainstorm for fundamentally faster sampling, exact first-jump sampling, which computes the probabilities of the first error in a block during the dominant pass instead of proposing and rejecting, needs 1.04 to 4.7 times fewer passes on small registers at a kernel cost of about a fifth of a pass ([Directions beyond the current algorithm](studies/new-directions.md)).

## Key results

- noisecirc's exact rejection sampling of non-mixed-unitary channels with state-independent operator-norm proposals was not found in the literature; depth-first prefix reuse was (SANQ, TUSQ, Pauli noise only). Partially verified. [Prior art](studies/prior-art.md)
- Block passes are predicted by a dry run of noisecirc's sampler within 0.89 to 1.12 times (30 GPU runs), by a closed form within 0.70 to 1.93 times. Verified. [Block passes](studies/pass-count.md)
- At equal expected errors per shot, the passes per block and shot are the same function for all six circuits and, within about ±40%, for five noise types. Verified on dry runs. [Block passes](studies/pass-count.md)
- Time over passes times $t_{bw}$: 1.24 to 2.58 (geometric mean 1.81) at defaults, 1.29 to 1.84 with `max_operations` = 32, which costs no time overall. Verified, H100, damping, 24 to 28 qubits. [Block-pass time](studies/block-pass-time.md)
- Benchmark circuits fall into three fusion categories (chain, width-limited, gate-count-limited); at equal error load the passes hardly depend on the circuit or the non-mixed-unitary channel. Verified on dry runs and GPU runs. [Benchmark circuits](studies/benchmark-suite.md)
- GPU runs of 7 circuits × 5 channels at 28 qubits confirm the predictions: passes within 0.86 to 1.13 times, times within 1.00 to 1.33 times (kernel-ratio model). Verified. [Validation](studies/validation.md)
- noisecirc is 6 to 52 times faster than Aer's shot branching and 27 to 7700 times faster than per-shot trajectories (Aer, CUDA-Q) at 24 qubits; almost all of it is algorithmic: wide fusion of noisy gates (4 to 25 times), sharing of trajectories (2 to 270 times) and no norm passes (1.5 times), while the implementations are within a factor of 2 at equal algorithm. At 28 qubits Aer's shot branching runs out of device memory from 0.1 expected errors per shot. Verified, amplitude damping. [Competitors](studies/competitors.md)
- Tuned Aer: a fusion width of 3 qubits (default 2 with noise) is Aer's only helpful option, saving 8% on average and up to 28% at one error per shot, because wider operations carry more Kraus matrices to compute norms for; with both simulators tuned, noisecirc is 6 to 48 times faster than Aer's shot branching at 24 qubits (67 where Aer's fusion fails) and 10 to 30 times at 28 qubits. Verified. [Aer tuning](studies/aer-tuning.md)
- cuStateVec Ex's delayed lower-bound sampling, the fastest per-shot simulator found (7 to 15 times faster than CUDA-Q and Aer per shot), is 4 to 520 times slower than noisecirc: sharing trajectories gives 15 to 270 times at loads of 0.01 to 0.1 (2.3 to 3.7 at one error per shot), wider fusion and kernels 1.6 to 3.2 times per trajectory. Verified. [Lower-bound sampling](studies/lower-bound-sampling.md)
- Masked kernels, which run the no-error code in every stage but those a trajectory's error is in, make noisecirc 4 to 13% faster and remove the noise dependence of its best block size; per-circuit autotuning of block size and stage width adds 0 to 14% (6% on average). Verified, H100, damping. [Error kernels](studies/error-kernels.md)
- noisecirc's parameters tuned on the RTX 4060 Ti are close to the best on the H100: the best uniform change (`max_operations` 64, `group_amplitudes` 4096) saves 4% on average, up to 14% on brickwork. Verified. [Parameter tuning](studies/parameter-tuning.md)
- Valid checkpoint-dependent proposal bounds save at most 6% of the passes with information the passes already provide (QFT; 1 to 4% elsewhere), and even perfect bounds only 4 to 36%: the rejected error proposals are mostly inherent to proposing without the state. Dry-run estimate. [Tighter bounds](studies/tighter-bounds.md)
- The canonical Kraus set maximises the dominant operator's weight (the "most dominant first" rule is the canonical decomposition); the optimal set keeps it and minimises the others' oversampling to a certified lower bound, for every weighting of error proposals against accepted errors. It is the canonical set for amplitude damping; for channels with a mixed-unitary part it makes 1.2 to 2 times fewer error proposals. Verified numerically for seven channels on one and two qubits. [Kraus representations](studies/kraus-representation.md) Counting shots that propose the same error as one pass, the sequential rule (most dominant first, then the most dominant of the rest) is provably optimal for the distinct accepted errors at every shot count; among the minimal-oversampling sets the most concentrated is best, but in dry runs this changes passes by at most a few percent.
- Pass time on the H100 fits $\max(1.17, 0.90\,F/F_{bw} + 0.81\,S/S_{bw})$ times $t_{bw}$ within 12%: arithmetic and shared accesses add. `complex32` alone saves 15 to 29% of a run, larger blocks at most 15%. Verified. [Instruction-bound pass](studies/kernel-regime.md) Disassembled kernels also issue 35 to 90 integer instructions per amplitude (addresses, per-stage setup), each costing about 0.85 of a floating-point one; immediate addresses and stages that ignore diagonal gates are predicted to save 1 to 13% on top of the best configuration (not timed: GPU 0 unavailable); half-precision arithmetic would bias norms by up to 4%. Applying each stage in its cheapest contraction order, found exactly as a shortest path over the down-sets of its dependency graph, saves 8 to 18% of the arithmetic of brickwork, grid and QAOA over the A14 kernels and is predicted to shorten their passes by up to 4% (verified on the CPU, not timed).
- In-stage fusion and real rotations between deferred diagonals cut arithmetic by 18 to 75% at single-precision accuracy and runs by 0 to 11%; with 4-qubit stages and `complex32`, runs are 1.45 to 1.9 times faster (accuracy about $10^{-3}$). Verified, 28 qubits. [Fewer instructions](studies/block-instructions.md)
- Tensor cores (`mma.sync`, H100) do not speed up block passes at single precision: tensor-core stages with three-product TF32 or FP16 splits (`tf32x3`, `f16x3`) are as accurate as `complex64` CUDA-core kernels but 15% to 2.6 times slower; a single FP16 product makes brickwork passes 4 to 8% faster at an accuracy of about $10^{-3}$, worse than `complex32` storage. Measured peaks: FP16 483, TF32 242, FP32 FMA 41.7 TFLOP/s. Verified, five circuits at 26 qubits, pass times. [Tensor cores](studies/tensor-cores.md)
- First-jump sampling is exact and needs 1.04 to 3.3 times fewer passes at up to one error per shot and 1.7 to 4.7 at three (10 qubits, real states), at about +20% per pass for the reductions; the readout map and peeling the last block save about 1.5 times for GHZ and a few percent elsewhere. Reference implementation and dry runs. [New directions](studies/new-directions.md)
- svpass's single-warp norm reduction made 28-qubit runs up to 1.7 times slower; fixed on `fix/squared-norms-parallel`. Verified. [Issues](studies/noisecirc-issues.md)
- `optimize_kraus` spread the no-error Kraus operator and filled the kernels' zero entries, making depolarizing runs up to 10 times slower; fixed on `fix/optimize-kraus-dominant`, after which it saves 20 to 30% at $M = 1$. Verified. [Issues](studies/noisecirc-issues.md)
